Bug Catalogue

A tap-game creative-artifact in the form of a 19th-century entomologist's display case. The user is shown an empty pinning board for 30 seconds while pixel-art bugs of wildly invented species crawl/fly across the screen at varying speeds and trajectories — a gold-banded clock-roach, a translucent moth with stained-glass wings, a tiny six-legged thing carrying a cigarette. Tap a bug to pin it (gentle squelch sound, paper-thump). Catch up to 6. At time-up, the user's caught specimens snap into a riso-printed museum collection plate: each insect labeled with a deterministic latin binomial seeded from the species id and the user's catch-time, plus a one-line deadpan field note from the curator ("Specimen 03 — *Coleoptera misericordia*, captured 14:32 — known to overshadow conversations about feelings"). The plate IS the share artifact: stained sepia paper, hand-engraved illustration style, brass corner stamps. No verdict, no quiz; the catalogue is a personal museum the user assembled. Daily seed determines which species appear today, so two players' boards differ-but-rhyme.
