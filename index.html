Heist Plotter

A mock after-action heist generator. The screen shows a tiny pixel-art floor plan (vault / penthouse / museum / chocolate factory). The user clicks 5 numbered waypoints in sequence to plot the route — entry, distraction, target, switch-back, exfil. The path itself is the input. On submit, the Bureau of Notable Capers issues a tri-folded after-action poster: a named alias ("Maestro of the Rear Stairwell", "The Lobby Whisperer"), a route map of the user's exact path as Exhibit A, a loot tally derived from path geometry (zigzag length, time near guards, doors crossed), three deadpan named accomplices, and a CASE: CLOSED stamp with verdict ("Clean", "Botched", "Legendary").

Koi Coin Rally

A 60-second kawaii pixel-art race where you ride a chibi koi fish surfing down a winding river race-track. Tilt left/right with arrow keys (desktop) or tap left/right halves of the screen (mobile) to weave between lily pads, dodge cranky frogs, and scoop up gold coins. Three lanes, a single continuous river, a finish-line gate. The coin total at the finish converts deterministically into a named pilot license — 'Koi Cadet Sumiko (47 coins)', 'Reckless Sushi Express (88 coins)', 'Lord Mochi of the Coursed Stream (120+)'. Daily seeded race-track means everyone gets the same course today; share card is your fish portrait + license title + coin emoji-grid for the run.

Catch of the Day

A 30-second pixel-art fishing minigame: tap to cast, then a tension meter wobbles as a hidden fish fights the line — release at the right moment, mistime it twice and the fish breaks free. Six measurable catch traits (cast distance, tension peaks, fight duration, line-snap near-misses, reel rhythm, time-of-bite) deterministically condition both a named species and an illustrated portrait. The reveal is a tri-folded Naturalist's Field Plate from the fictional 'Lake Verity Survey' — generated fish illustration centered as Exhibit A in 1830s scientific-plate style (etched cross-hatching, faded sepia paper, latin binomial), with HTML/CSS overlays for the species name ('Glass-Toothed Drift Carp'), behavioral notes quoting your specific catch ('fought for 7s with two surface breaches'), conservation status, and a deadpan field-naturalist's quote.

The Bullwhip

A solo, two-minute Beer Game run rendered as four pixel-art warehouses stacked vertically — Retailer, Distributor, Wholesaler, Factory — connected by a conveyor of cardboard cases. The user picks one tier on the title card; the other three are AI agents driven by an anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic. Each tick the user taps how many cases to order from upstream. A hidden customer-demand curve has one small bump at week 3. After 20 weeks the Bureau of Logistical Conduct issues a deadpan Bullwhip Audit: a ripple chart of orders across all four tiers, peak inventory, stockout weeks, and a named manager archetype. Same demand seed for everyone today; archetype keyed off (variance, lag, peak-inventory). Share replays the exact 20 ticks via URL fragment.

Tank Verdict

A nano-aquarium personality oracle. The tank is already running when you land — six tiny pixel fish darting through driftwood and a slow algae bloom on a dark glass canvas. You don't pet them, you don't feed them: you just watch for 60 seconds while a hidden ethologist scores six visible behaviors (shoaling tightness, surface-vs-bottom dwelling, glass-pacing, hierarchy fights, hide-out time, food-chase ferocity). At the end the tank is named and the dominant fish is crowned with a deadpan biotope-society plaque: 'The Glass-Pacing Dynasty', 'A Quiet Zen Pool', 'The Shrimp Mafia'. Each session reseeds (hash of timestamp + a 'shake the tank' button to reseed manually) so reruns visibly behave differently. Share is a screenshot of the labeled tank-as-portrait with the verdict caption — no dialog, the tab IS the share. Aquascape aesthetic: minimalist black-water biotope, off-register risograph greens, single warm light from above.
